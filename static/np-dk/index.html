Newcomers to a new generation:
the story of Nepali Denmark

In 2008, 1,174 people of Nepali origin lived in Denmark. By the latest count (2026) they numbered 15,408 — a roughly thirteenfold increase in under two decades, powered above all by students and a young, Danish-born second generation.

A flat decade, then a surge

For its first ten years the community grew steadily but gently. Between 2018 and 2020 it stalled completely — then, from 2021 onward, the curve bent sharply upward, adding more people in the last five years than in all the years before combined.

Nine in ten come to study

The surge has a clear engine. Statbank also classifies immigration by the residence permit people arrive on — and among Nepali citizens it is overwhelmingly study. In 2025, of 3,073 immigrants, 2,804 (about 91%) came on a study-related permit. The student pipeline is Nepali migration to Denmark — a pattern visible as far back as 2007.

Source: Statistics Denmark — Immigrations (year) by citizenship, sex and residence permit

A second, smaller channel has appeared alongside study. Nepali citizens admitted under EU/EEA rules are almost entirely dependent family members — spouses and children of an EU/EEA national — rather than EU workers or students. That group was in single digits before 2019, then jumped to 454 in 2022. It looks like family reunification following the students, not a separate labour migration.

Immigrants today, Danish-born Nepalis tomorrow

Everyone in this data is either an immigrant (born abroad) or a descendant (born in Denmark to Nepali parents). Descendants started as a rounding error — 64 people in 2008 — but they are the fastest-growing share, the beginnings of a settled Nepali-Danish generation.

Becoming Danish

Immigration is only half the story. As people put down roots, some take the final step: Danish citizenship. It stayed a trickle for decades — barely a handful a year — but has climbed since 2020, reaching 114 in 2024. In all, 693 people of Nepali origin have acquired Danish citizenship since 1979.

Data & method

All figures come from Statistics Denmark's Statbank. The population stock is table FOLK1C — population at the first day of the quarter by country of origin, age, sex, ancestry and region. The annual flows come from separate tables: Immigrations (year) by citizenship, sex and residence permit (1997–2025) and Persons who acquired Danish citizenship by former citizenship, sex and age (1979–2025). For the stock table the query selects country of origin = Nepal, all age groups, both sexes, both ancestry categories (immigrants and descendants; "persons of Danish origin" are structurally zero for this origin), and every region and municipality.

Time: every year-point is a Q4 (year-end) observation, except the latest point, 2026, which is Q3. The raw download was split into three batches to respect Statbank's 100,000-record limit and merged with scripts/merge_statbank.py.
